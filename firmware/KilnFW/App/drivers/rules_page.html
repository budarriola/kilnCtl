A relay marked DRIVEN 1 is evaluated and driven automatically by the firmware's rule-evaluator task, once per second, through the same ownership arbitration a running profile uses -- it cannot fight a manual relay command or a profile that already owns that relay (whichever claims a relay first keeps it; see the live table below for which relays are currently rule-owned). A relay a running profile or autotune test already owns is left alone by the rule engine even if marked DRIVEN 1, and never left on if the safety link drops or an ESP/Pico update is in progress -- both force every rule-driven relay off.

A relay assigned to any zone on the Thermocouples & Zones page is a PID/heater relay and can never be rule_driven -- the firmware refuses RELAY <n> DRIVEN 1 for it (see the "Heater/PID" column in the live table below for which relays that is, right now). It may still be referenced as a RELAY condition in another relay's rule -- rules may read a heater relay's state, they just may not command it. Rules are meant to drive non-PID auxiliary hardware: reduction flame, vents, blowers.

Hand-typed format, one line each. A relay's rules are OR'd together; a rule's conditions are AND'd together.

RELAY <n 1-4> DRIVEN <0|1>
R <rule 0-2> TEMP <zone 0-2> <GE|LE> <threshold_c>
R <rule 0-2> TIME <GE|LE> <seconds>
R <rule 0-2> RELAY <other 1-4> <0|1>

Example: vent relay 2 opens once zone 0 passes 500C
  RELAY 2 DRIVEN 1
  R 0 TEMP 0 GE 500.0

Relay contact wear

Lifetime on/off transitions per relay, counted by the control loop and kept across reboots. These are electromechanical relays: their contacts are good for roughly 100,000 operations, so a number climbing toward six figures means that relay is nearing the end of its life and should be replaced before it welds shut during a firing.
